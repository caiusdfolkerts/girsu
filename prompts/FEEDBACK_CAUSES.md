When a mistake is reported, do not merely change the returned field and move on. Check the finding against the exact original and record, understand what caused the mistake, repair other work exposed to that cause, and change the approach that produced it. ## Respond to feedback and prevent recurrence

Read `appendix/worked_sources/RETURNS_AND_LESSONS.md` (shared returns and dispositions; missing from this extract) at a new batch boundary and obtain the current authorized snapshot when working live. The append-only `cf_events` ledger/current exact decisions are authoritative; the supplied appendix is a dated navigation snapshot, not a new registry.

Only adjudicated generally applicable lessons are general rules. Read later dispositions: a raw return may be partly wrong, disputed or withdrawn.

First decide whether the finding is right. Inspect its source evidence, the actual encoded assertion and allowed equivalent representations. If it is wrong, explain the exact evidence or structural path and request correction of the finding; do not damage sound data to satisfy it.

A partly wrong review can still contain legitimate findings. Keep those findings separate, and preserve later withdrawals or disputes rather than treating every historical return as a rule.

For a confirmed error, identify the most likely cause: an overlooked clause, a mistaken interpretation, a helper/default, unclear instructions, or another specific mechanism. Fix the responsible construction or reasoning method, not just this output.

Search the relevant work that used the same pattern, including accessible drafts, submissions, returns and accepted versions. Check each candidate before changing it. Bound this search by the cause; do not launch a general corpus audit for an isolated slip. State any affected stages or versions you cannot inspect instead of claiming they are clear.

Make the smallest supported repairs through the normal predecessor-bound submission and independent-review workflow. Preserve the original submissions and exact differences.

Share confirmed generally applicable corrections through the assigned feedback path so other workers can check relevant work; do not spread a disputed interpretation or a false return as a universal instruction. A shared tool or instruction defect needs a reproducible example and the responsible owner's correction, not an unrequested infrastructure rewrite.

Carry the corrected method into the next comparable sources and respond to what independent review finds.

Repairing the exposed example or acknowledging a lesson does not by itself show that the cause has stopped recurring. Do not call a draft written before feedback a repeat after feedback merely because it was reviewed later. Keep the ordinary cause note short: cause and evidence, affected work, repairs, and anything still pending.

Keep useful work moving. Complete immediate repairs and preserve pending checks, then continue unrelated ready sources while review or another responsible worker finishes its part. Keep corrections in the ordinary work-and-review flow rather than waiting for publication.

A source-specific mistake and a faulty shared parser need different-sized responses; both need a real correction rather than an assurance.
